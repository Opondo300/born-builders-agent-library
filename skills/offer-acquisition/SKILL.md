# Offer & Acquisition Frameworks — Hormozi

Source: $100M Offers, $100M Leads (Alex Hormozi)

---

## The Value Equation

```
Value = (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement)
        ÷ (Time Delay × Effort & Sacrifice)
```

**To increase value — increase the numerator OR decrease the denominator:**

| Lever | How to Increase | Common Mistake |
|-------|----------------|----------------|
| Dream Outcome | Make it more specific and vivid. "$411/day per vacant SIL bed" > "improve revenue" | Vague aspirational language |
| Perceived Likelihood | Add proof: testimonials, case studies, data, guarantees | Claiming outcomes without evidence |
| Time Delay | Shorten time to first result. What is the fastest win? | Promising long-term results with no near-term signal |
| Effort & Sacrifice | Reduce what the customer has to do. Done-for-you > DIY | Making the customer do too much work |

**Diagnostic:** If an offer is not converting, identify which lever is weakest. Fix that lever before changing anything else.

## Grand Slam Offer — 4-Element Structure

An offer so good, people feel stupid saying no. All four must be present:

1. **High Dream Outcome** — What they desperately want. Specific, vivid, quantified.
2. **High Perceived Likelihood** — Why they will actually get it. Proof + guarantee.
3. **Low Time Delay** — How fast they see results. First win in days, not months.
4. **Low Effort & Sacrifice** — How little they have to do. You do the work.

## Grand Slam Offer — 9-Component Build

1. **Market Analysis** — Identify the "starving crowd." Demand > supply. Offer to a cold audience first; if it converts cold, it converts everywhere.
2. **Value-Based Pricing** — Price on the value delivered, not on cost or competitor comparison.
3. **Product Packaging** — Bundle solutions, not features. Each element addresses a specific problem.
4. **Value Proposition Design** — One specific transformation. Not a list of deliverables.
5. **Scalable Business Model** — The offer must work at volume without linear cost increase.
6. **Sales Incentives** — Urgency and scarcity built in. Deadlines must be real.
7. **Guarantees** — Remove the risk from the buyer. Types: results guarantee, money-back, service guarantee, anti-guarantee ("keep everything").
8. **Closing Tactics** — Price anchoring, payment plans, payment timing.
9. **Strategic Naming** — M.A.G.I.C. formula (see below).

## Market Selection — 4 Traits of a Starving Crowd

A market worth entering has all four:

1. **Massive pain** — They are desperate for a solution. Not nice-to-have.
2. **Purchasing power** — They have money to spend on the problem.
3. **Easy to target** — You can find and reach them specifically.
4. **Growing** — The market is expanding, not shrinking.

**Rule:** Do not enter a market missing any of these. A great offer in a bad market loses.

## Problem-to-Solution Methodology (Offer Creation Process)

1. Identify the dream outcome in the client's own words
2. List every problem standing between them and the outcome (20+ problems)
3. Turn each problem into a solution ("I struggle with X" → "X solution")
4. Assign a delivery vehicle to each solution (1:1, group, DIY, done-for-you, software, etc.)
5. Bundle the highest-value solutions into the offer
6. Trim and stack: keep only solutions that move the needle; eliminate the rest

## Core Four — Lead Generation Methods

Every business has exactly four ways to get leads:

| Method | Description | Speed | Cost | Best For |
|--------|-------------|-------|------|----------|
| **Warm Outreach** | Contact people you know | Fast | Free | Starting out, reactivations |
| **Cold Outreach** | Contact strangers directly | Medium | Low | Scalable, targeted |
| **Content** | Publish to attract inbound | Slow | Low | Long-term compounding |
| **Paid Ads** | Buy attention | Fast | High | Scaling proven offers |

**Rule of 100:** Do 100 actions per day in your chosen method for 100 days before judging it. Most people quit at 2 weeks.

**More/Better/New Framework (Scaling Sequence):**
1. **More** — Do more of what is already working. Increase volume before changing anything.
2. **Better** — Improve conversion at each stage. Only after volume is proven.
3. **New** — Add new channels or offers. Only after More and Better are exhausted.

Most operators skip to New when they should be doing More.

## Lead Magnet Formula

`[Number] + [Adjective] + [Target Audience] + [Desired Outcome] + [Time Frame]`

Example: "5 Proven Scripts NDIS SIL Providers Use to Book 3 New Coordinator Meetings Per Week"

Lead magnets that work: checklists, templates, calculators, short trainings, audits, swipe files.
Lead magnets that don't work: ebooks nobody reads, generic guides, anything requiring more than 10 minutes.

## Proof-Promise-Plan Framework (Sales Pitch Structure)

Use this to structure any sales conversation or pitch:

1. **Proof (1–2 min)** — Establish credibility. Specific results, not credentials. "We helped a SIL provider go from 3 to 8 coordinator relationships in 90 days."
2. **Promise (2–3 min)** — State the specific transformation using the Value Equation. Vivid, quantified.
3. **Plan (1 min)** — 3 manageable steps that make success feel achievable. Simple. They own it.

## M.A.G.I.C. Naming Formula

Every offer needs a name that sells:

- **M**ake a Magnetic Reason — Why now? What forces the action?
- **A**vatar — Who specifically is this for?
- **G**oal — What is the specific outcome?
- **I**nterval — How long does it take?
- **C**ontainer — What is the vehicle? (System, Program, Blueprint, Protocol)

## Guarantee Types

| Type | Mechanics | Use When |
|------|-----------|----------|
| **Results guarantee** | Achieve X outcome or money back | Outcome is measurable |
| **Service guarantee** | We will keep working until it works | Outcome is partially dependent on client |
| **Anti-guarantee** | "Keep everything even if you refund" | Info products, low-cost offers |
| **Time-based** | Results within N days or refund | Speed is the key value driver |

**Rule:** The guarantee should be so specific it scares you to offer it. Generic guarantees mean nothing.

---

## Diagnostic Questions (Apply When Reviewing Any Offer)

- Which of the 4 Value Equation levers is weakest?
- Is this a starving crowd or a comfortable crowd?
- What specific result is guaranteed, and by when?
- What does the customer NOT have to do that they normally would?
- Is there a real deadline or manufactured urgency?
- Can someone explain this offer in one sentence?
