# Hook Engine

**What this skill adds** — `copywriting-craft` covers appeal hierarchy, awareness levels, specificity, and structure. `attention-belief` covers the neuroscience of attention gates, information gap theory, and Zeigarnik. This skill does not repeat those. It covers what they don't: the production system for generating hooks, the format-switching framework, and the derived rules that cross all of them.

---

## When to Invoke

Before writing any hook — headline, email subject, video first line, carousel cover, static ad copy, or cold email opener.

---

## The Derived Ruleset

Eight rules derived from first principles. They override intuition every time intuition and the rules conflict.

**Rule 1: A hook is not a clever line — it is a prediction violation.**
The brain's cortex fires (P300) when the next thing violates what it modelled was coming. "Clever" produces admiration. Prediction violation produces compulsive attention. These are different. The test: does this sentence say what the audience expects a sentence in this context to say? If yes, it is not a hook.

**Rule 2: Complete information kills the hook. Incomplete information kills the conversion.**
The Loewenstein information gap functions as a dopaminergic drive state — but only when partial information is present. A vague question produces no gap. A complete answer produces no curiosity. Maximum drive state sits at approximately 50% revelation. Error mode A (too vague): "Here's the mistake most people make." Error mode B (too complete): leading with the answer, then explaining. Thread the middle: name the territory, withhold the map.

**Rule 3: A strong hook with weak body is the worst algorithmic outcome — not a neutral one.**
This is the most commonly violated assumption. TikTok's data (Kaggle Viral Shorts: hook strength 0.95, completion rate 22%) confirms that baiting attention without delivering on it is penalised harder than never baiting it at all. The hook is a contract with the algorithm and the reader simultaneously. Write the body first. Hook second.

**Rule 4: Identity beats information. Always.**
Information hooks have a half-life. Identity hooks do not. "Do You Make These Mistakes in English?" (Sackheim, 1919) ran for 40 years. Nike "Just Do It" is now 37 years old. The mechanism: identity is not consumed. Information is consumed and depreciates. When the audience's identity can be activated — who they see themselves as, who they're trying to become, who they don't want to be — that hook outlasts any statistic or fact. Check: does this hook sell them information about the world, or does it sell them a version of themselves?

**Rule 5: Customer language beats copywriter language. Every time.**
Wiebe's "I smell like a CEO now" (from a 3-star Harley-Davidson review) doubled CTR and halved CPC. The mechanism: the reader's brain recognises its own language before it recognises new language. Mine the 3-star review (specific frustration without total abandonment) — not the 5-star (enthusiasm without tension) and not the 1-star (anger without actionability). The 3-star contains the exact cognitive frame of a prospect who is aware of the problem and hasn't yet decided. That language is the hook.

**Rule 6: The first 5 variants are always the obvious ones. They are worth writing and worth discarding.**
Halbert wrote on physical cards, shuffled them, discarded. Upworthy ran 25 minimum per piece from 32,487 experiments. The implication is not "write more." It is: the first 5 variants are where the obvious ideas drain. Variant 6 onward is where differentiation begins. Do not ship a hook from the first 5. Write 15. Kill the first 5. Choose from the remainder.

**Rule 7: Video has two hooks. They are not the same hook.**
Content hook: what the viewer will get. Story hook: what is at stake. A video with only a content hook loses viewers at 30 seconds when curiosity is satisfied. A video with only a story hook loses viewers who don't yet care about the character. Both must be present in the first 30 seconds. Thumbnail + title carry the content hook before play. First 30 seconds carries the story hook.

**Rule 8: The format sets the pre-attentive constraint, not the creative constraint.**
Static: color and contrast fire at 50ms (Lindgaard 2006) — the gist is set before a single word is read. The visual IS the hook. Words confirm it, they do not make it. Carousel: the cover slide hook is compressed because two impressions are available per post (Mosseri's re-show mechanic — non-swipers get served from slide 2). Video: the TikTok decision window is 0.3–0.8 seconds, not 1.7 seconds (the 1.7s figure is Meta's; TikTok's own data is faster). Email subject: 2–4 words at 46% open rate (Belkins, 5.5M B2B emails); personalization increases opens 31% and replies 133%. Written copy: the headline is 80% of the ad (Ogilvy); the opener's single job is pulling the eye to line 2, not explaining anything.

## The Production Workflow

**Step 1 — VOC Mine before writing.**
Pull 15–20 reviews, forum posts, or customer quotes in the exact category. Find the 3-star entries. Extract the phrase that is most specific to the frustration. That phrase is the raw material. Don't paraphrase it — use it.

**Step 2 — Identify the awareness level** (from `copywriting-craft`: Schwartz's 5 levels).
Most aware → Short, direct, offer-first. Problem aware → Lead with the exact problem named in their language. Unaware → Story-first, never feature-first.

**Step 3 — Write the body. Then write the hook.**
The hook is a promise the body must keep. Writing the hook first produces a mismatch 70% of the time and algorithmic penalty when deployed. Write the core argument or content first. The hook emerges from knowing what it needs to promise.

**Step 4 — Write 15 variants. Discard the first 5.**
Variant formats to cycle through: number (specificity), loss frame (2.25x weight vs. gain — Kahneman/Tversky), identity activation, prediction violation, VOC verbatim, contrarian position, and implied transformation. One variant per frame. None should share a mechanism.

**Step 5 — Apply the format constraint.**
Run the format check from the framework below. The hook that survived Step 4 may need recompression for the format's pre-attentive window.

**Step 6 — Evaluate against Rule 3.**
Does the body deliver what the hook implies? If not, rewrite the body or rewrite the hook. Do not ship the mismatch.

## Format-Switching Framework

| Format | Pre-attentive window | Hook length | Primary mechanism | Common failure |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Video (TikTok/Reels) | 0.3–0.8 seconds | First spoken sentence + visual | Pattern interrupt + story hook | Content hook only; no stakes |
| Video (YouTube) | Thumbnail + title + first 30s | Title under 60 chars; first 30s deliver story hook | Two-hook structure (content + story) | Hook delivers; retention collapses at 30s |
| Carousel (Instagram) | Cover slide, re-served at slide 2 | 6–8 words on cover; shorter than written copy | Zeigarnik (open loop sustained across slides) | Resolving the hook on the cover slide |
| Static ad / graphic | 50ms visual gist | Visual IS the hook; headline confirms | Color/contrast gist → headline specificity | Headline doing the work the visual must do |
| Email subject line | 2–4 words at peak | Under 40 characters | Information gap + personalization | "Urgent" framing (−23% in B2B); urgency without relevance |
| LinkedIn post | First 1.5 lines before "more" | Under 60 characters | Contrarian position or specific number | Starting with "I" or a compliment; soft opener |
| Cold email opener | Sentence 1 | Under 15 words | Self-relevance signal (specific to this person) | Restating industry pain; generic personalisation |
| Written copy (headline) | Pre-body scan | 80% of the ad; under 12 words for highest-performing | Self-interest + news or specificity | Curiosity alone (Caples' weakest appeal solo) |

## The Diagnostic

Before any hook ships, answer these in order. A "no" stops the process:

1. Does this violate a prediction the reader would have made in this context?
2. Does it reveal enough to open a gap without closing it?
3. Is the body strong enough to justify this hook?
4. Is this variant from the second half of the 15-variant set?
5. Does the language come from the customer or from the copywriter?
6. Does the format constraint match the pre-attentive window of the platform?
